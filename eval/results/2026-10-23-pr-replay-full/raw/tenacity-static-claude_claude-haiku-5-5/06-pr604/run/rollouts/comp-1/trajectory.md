final message:
I added `enabled` (default `True`) to `Retrying`, `AsyncRetrying` and `@retry`. With `enabled=False` the function runs once with no retry logic, and its exception or return value comes back unchanged. The full suite passes (148 passed, 1 skipped) and the stdlib checker reports 0 problems. The changes are uncommitted in the worktree.

**How it works**
- The retry loop is shared, so I put the check in `BaseRetrying._begin_iter`. When disabled, it skips `before`, `after`, `retry`, `wait`, `stop` and `before_sleep`. It returns the attempt's result, or re-raises its exception. This one branch covers `__call__`, `for`/`async for` iteration, and the Tornado path.
- `enabled` is also threaded through `copy()`, so `fn.retry_with(...)` keeps it, and through `AsyncRetrying.__init__` and the two `retry()` overloads.

**Choices I made that you may want to change**
- With `enabled=False`, `for attempt in Retrying(...)` runs the body once. If it raises, the exception surfaces after the `with` block, not inside it.
- `before` callbacks are skipped when disabled, not just the retry decision.
